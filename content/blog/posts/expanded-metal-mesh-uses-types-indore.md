Expanded metal mesh is a strange product. It looks woven but is not. It is not welded either. It is made from a single sheet of steel that is slit and stretched, so the metal opens into a diamond pattern without a single join.

That is where its strength comes from. There are no welds to break and no wires to slip.

## How it is made

A sheet of mild steel passes through a machine that makes slits in a staggered pattern while pulling the sheet open. The slits become diamond holes. The strands stay joined at every corner because they were never cut apart.

The result is a rigid, one-piece mesh that weighs less than the sheet it came from.

## Standard and flattened

- **Standard (raised).** The strands sit at an angle. It is stiffer, has a rougher surface, and gives some privacy and shade when seen from the side.
- **Flattened.** Rolled flat after expansion. It is smoother, gives a flat face and lets more light through.

Raised and flattened are different products with different thickness and opening figures. Order one or the other, not "expanded mesh" alone.

## Where it is used

| Use | Why expanded mesh |
|---|---|
| Plaster and render | The diamond holes key the plaster and help control cracking |
| Gates, grills, window guards | Harder to cut through than light wire, and hard to see through |
| Fences and security panels | Rigid, with no wire to unpick |
| Walkways, platforms, steps | Can give grip and drain water, but only in the right grade, strand size, support and design. Ask a structural engineer |
| Machine guards | See through; suitability depends on the opening size, strand and fixing the guard design requires |
| Cable trays and racks | Light, ventilated |
| False ceiling grids and screens | Neat, with open area |
| Cooler panels | Cheap and robust |

## How to specify it

Four things describe the mesh:

1. **Strand thickness** and width, in mm.
2. **Diamond size**: the long and short way of the diamond (LWD and SWD). These are measured centre to centre of the strands, like a pitch, so the clear opening is smaller. Say which you mean.
3. **Sheet or roll size**.
4. **Material**: mild steel, galvanised, aluminium or stainless.

If you have a sample, bring it. Measure the long way of the diamond with a ruler and tell us the thickness of the steel.

## Expanded mesh vs welded mesh vs wire mesh

| | Expanded | Welded | Woven wire |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joins | None | Welded points | Woven crossings |
| Rigidity | High | High | Low to medium |
| Weight per area | Light | Medium | Light |
| Best for | Plaster, guards, grills | Fencing, cages, slabs | Screens, sieves |

If the job needs a flat, rigid, one-piece panel, expanded mesh is hard to beat. If it must be flexible and rolled, woven wire is better. For slabs and fences, see [welded mesh](/categories/welded-mesh).

## Paint or galvanise

Plain mild steel mesh rusts outdoors. For external use, ask for galvanised, or paint it with a primer and enamel before it goes up.

## FAQs

**What is expanded metal mesh used for?**  
Gates, grills, guards, plaster reinforcement, walkways and fences.

**Is expanded mesh stronger than wire mesh?**  
It is more rigid, because it is a single piece with no weld points. Which is stronger for a job depends on the strand size, the material and the design.

**Can expanded mesh be galvanised?**  
Yes, it is available galvanised for outdoor use.

**What is the difference between standard and flattened?**  
Standard has angled strands and a rough surface. Flattened has a flat face and is smoother.

**Do you sell expanded mesh by the roll or by the sheet?**  
Both. Tell us the size.
