**Quick answer:** A higher gauge number means thinner wire. 12 SWG is 2.64 mm, 14 SWG is 2.03 mm, 16 SWG is 1.63 mm and 18 SWG is 1.22 mm. Rebar tying uses 16 to 18 SWG annealed binding wire. Fence tying uses 12 to 14 SWG GI.

You ask for 12 gauge wire. The shop hands you a coil. How do you know it is the 12 gauge you meant?

Because two gauge systems are in use in India, "12 gauge" can mean two different thicknesses. Add the fact that a bigger gauge number means a thinner wire, and you can see where mistakes begin.

## The rule that confuses everyone

**The higher the gauge number, the thinner the wire.** 8 gauge is thick fencing wire. 20 gauge is thin tie wire.

## SWG and BWG in millimetres

India mainly uses SWG (Standard Wire Gauge, also called Imperial). BWG (Birmingham Wire Gauge) appears on some tube and steel wire specs. They are close but not the same.

| Gauge | SWG (mm) | BWG (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 4.88 | 5.16 |
| 8 | 4.06 | 4.19 |
| 10 | 3.25 | 3.40 |
| 12 | 2.64 | 2.77 |
| 14 | 2.03 | 2.11 |
| 16 | 1.63 | 1.65 |
| 18 | 1.22 | 1.24 |
| 20 | 0.91 | 0.89 |
| 22 | 0.71 | 0.71 |

Always confirm which gauge system your supplier uses, or ask for the diameter in millimetres. A micrometer or vernier caliper settles it in seconds.

## What gauge for what job

| Job | Common choice |
|---|---|
| Rebar tying (binding wire) | 16 to 18 SWG, annealed |
| Compound wall and fence tying | 12 to 14 SWG GI |
| Chain link tension wire | 8 to 10 SWG GI |
| Barbed wire | 12 to 14 SWG, two strands twisted |
| Jhatka machine wire | 12 to 14 SWG high tensile |
| Binding for mesh and cages | 18 to 20 SWG |
| Heavy tie-downs, hanging | 8 to 10 SWG GI |

These are the usual ranges. A designer or the project specification can ask for something different.

## GI, black and annealed: what the finish means

- **GI (galvanised) wire** has a zinc coating. It lasts outdoors. See [GI wire](/products/gi-wire).
- **Black annealed wire** is soft, dark and easy to twist. It is the standard binding wire for rebar. See [binding wire](/products/binding-wire).
- **High tensile wire** is hard and springy. It is used for jhatka and tension lines, not tying.

Annealing is the heat treatment that makes the wire soft. A coil that feels stiff and springy is not binding wire however it is labelled.

## How to check a coil

1. **Measure the diameter** with a caliper in two places along a straight length.
2. **Pull a short piece to bend it**. Binding wire should twist cleanly without snapping.
3. **Look at the coating** on GI wire. Zinc should be smooth and even, not flaky.
4. **Check the weight** against the label. A coil short by a few kg is a short coil.

## Weight of wire per metre

Steel wire is a solid cylinder, so the same rule as for rebar applies: weight per metre is about d² ÷ 162 kg, where d is in millimetres. For 2.64 mm (12 SWG) that is about 0.043 kg per metre, so one kg is roughly 23 metres.

That means a 25 kg coil of 12 SWG wire holds about 575 metres. Use that when you compare coils from different suppliers. A galvanised coat adds a little.

## FAQs

**What is 12 gauge wire in mm?**  
2.64 mm in SWG and 2.77 mm in BWG.

**Is 16 gauge or 18 gauge binding wire better?**  
Both work. 16 gauge is stronger and 18 gauge is easier to twist by hand. Many sites use 18 gauge for small bars.

**What gauge is barbed wire?**  
Typically 12 to 14 gauge, in two strands twisted together with barbs.

**What is the difference between SWG and BWG?**  
They are two different measuring systems with slightly different diameters at each number. SWG is more common in India for wire.

**How much binding wire do I need per tonne of steel?**  
Sites commonly budget about 8 to 10 kg per tonne of rebar. Your engineer's bar schedule decides the real number.
