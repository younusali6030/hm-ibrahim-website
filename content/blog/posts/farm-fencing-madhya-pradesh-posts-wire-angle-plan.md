A farm fence is not about looks. It is about keeping cattle, nilgai, thieves and neighbours out for ten years without a repair bill every monsoon.

Most failures come from one of three mistakes: posts too far apart, wire too thin for the animal, or no plan for the corners. This guide sets out a plan you can hand to a contractor.

## Step 1: decide what the fence is for

| Threat | Fence that works |
|---|---|
| Stray cattle, goats | Barbed wire, 4 to 5 strands |
| Nilgai, deer | Chain link or welded mesh, 5 to 6 ft high |
| Thieves, boundary dispute | Chain link topped with barbed or concertina |
| Poultry, small animals | Chicken mesh or hexagonal mesh at the base |
| Crop protection from birds | Net over frame, not a fence |

## Step 2: pick the wire

- **[Barbed wire](/products/barbed-wire)** — The cheap, fast boundary. It marks a line and stops cattle. Not for nilgai, which jump.
- **[Jhatka machine wire](/products/jhatka-machine-fencing-wire)** — High tensile wire that carries a small charge from a jhatka machine. It holds off wild boar and nilgai without a tall fence.
- **[Chain link](/products/chain-link-fencing)** — Strong, tall and long lasting. The best all-round choice for a house plot or orchard.
- **[Welded mesh](/products/gi-weldmesh)** — Stiff panels or rolls for gates and enclosures.
- **[Concertina coil](/products/concertina-wire)** — For the top of a wall or for sites where security matters.

## Step 3: choose the posts

Posts decide how long the fence lives.

- **[MS angle](/products/ms-angles)** — The standard post. 40 x 40 or 50 x 50 mm for most farms. Angle takes holes for wire, is easy to weld, and is strong in the ground. Paint or galvanise it.
- **[FRP rods](/products/frp-bars)** — Light and rust free. Good for barbed wire and jhatka runs.
- **Concrete posts** — Good for the permanent compound.

A rough guide:

- **Line posts** every 3 to 3.5 metres for chain link, every 3 to 4 metres for barbed wire.
- **Corner and gate posts** heavier and set in concrete, with a diagonal brace.
- **Height** 7 feet of post for a 5 foot fence. Two feet goes into the ground.

## Step 4: build the corners first

Wire pulls. A corner post that leans will drag every post in the line with it. Set corners and ends in concrete, brace them diagonally, and strain the wire against them. Line posts are only guides.

## Step 5: tension and tie

Pull the wire tight with a strainer before you tie it. Tie it to the posts with [GI wire](/products/gi-wire) or [binding wire](/products/binding-wire). Slack wire is the first thing a cow leans on.

## FAQs

**What is the cheapest farm fence?**  
Barbed wire on a line of posts. It stops cattle but not nilgai.

**How far apart should fence posts be?**  
About 3 metres for chain link, 3 to 4 metres for barbed wire.

**What size angle iron for fence posts?**  
40 x 40 mm for light fences, 50 x 50 mm for tall chain link or exposed corners.

**Is a jhatka machine legal in India?**  
Fence energisers that give a low-power pulse are sold for farm use. Follow the maker's safety rules and local advice.
