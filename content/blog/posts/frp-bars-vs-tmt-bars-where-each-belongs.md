FRP bar is the product people ask about with the wrong question. They ask, "Is it stronger than TMT?" The better question is, "What problem am I trying to solve?"

FRP (fibre reinforced polymer) bar is made of glass fibre set in resin. It looks like a pale rod with a ribbed or sand-coated surface. It is not steel and it does not behave like steel. That is the point.

## What FRP does better

- **It does not rust.** There is no steel to corrode, so there is nothing to expand and crack the concrete from inside.
- **It is light.** About a quarter of the weight of steel for the same diameter. One person carries a bundle.
- **It does not conduct electricity** and does not interfere with magnetic or radio signals.
- **It resists salt and chemicals.** That matters near drains, tanks, marine work and chemical sheds.
- **Tensile strength is high.** Per unit area, glass FRP is stronger in tension than mild steel.

## What TMT does better

- **It bends on site.** TMT is cut and bent into stirrups and cranks with a bar bender. FRP bars cannot be bent on site. Bent shapes must be made at the factory.
- **It is ductile.** Steel yields and gives warning. FRP does not yield. It stretches elastically and then breaks suddenly.
- **It has a higher stiffness.** Glass FRP is less stiff than steel, so slabs reinforced with it deflect more and need a different design.
- **Standards are mature.** Design codes, sizes and test methods for TMT are well known to every site engineer.

## Where each belongs

| Job | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| House slab, beam, column | TMT | Familiar, ductile, on every drawing |
| Stirrups and bent shapes | TMT | FRP cannot be bent on site |
| Sewage, drain covers, septic tanks | FRP | No rust in wet, chemical conditions |
| Seawall, jetty, salty environments | FRP | Does not corrode |
| Compound wall pillars | FRP or TMT | Either works at this scale |
| Fence posts for barbed wire and mesh | FRP | Light, no rust, easy to carry and set |
| Near sensitive electrical equipment | FRP | Non-conductive |

## The fence post story

Much of the FRP we sell goes to farms. A 4, 5 or 6 foot FRP rod makes a post that does not rust in the soil, weighs next to nothing and is driven in by hand. Strung with [barbed wire](/products/barbed-wire) or [jhatka wire](/products/jhatka-machine-fencing-wire), it is a clean fence for orchards and fields. This is why FRP also sits in our [fencing material](/categories/fencing-material) range.

Common lengths and weights in our stock: 4 ft (about 530 g), 5 ft (about 660 g) and 6 ft (about 790 g).

## What to ask before you buy

1. **What is it for?** Reinforcement for a slab needs a structural design. A fence post does not.
2. **What diameter?** 6 mm to 20 mm. Thicker bars are for reinforcement. Thin ones for posts.
3. **Who designed it?** If it goes inside a building slab, your engineer must specify it.
4. **Which surface?** Sand-coated or ribbed bars bond better with concrete.

Never substitute FRP for steel in a structural member without the engineer's approval. The two materials are designed differently.

## FAQs

**Can FRP replace TMT in a house slab?**  
Not on its own decision. The slab must be designed for FRP, because it is less stiff and not ductile. Ask your structural engineer.

**Is FRP bar stronger than steel?**  
In tension, per unit area, glass FRP is stronger than mild steel. It is also less stiff and it does not yield, so strength alone is not the whole story.

**Does FRP rust?**  
No. It is made from glass fibre and resin.

**Can FRP bars be cut?**  
Yes, with a hacksaw or a cutting disc. Wear a mask because the dust irritates.

**What is FRP used for in farms?**  
Fence posts and crop supports. It does not rust in soil and it is light to carry.
