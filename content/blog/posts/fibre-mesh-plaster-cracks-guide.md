Look at the plaster on any building in Indore. Find the line where a brick wall meets a concrete beam or column. If there is a hairline crack running along it, you have found the most common plaster fault there is.

It is not bad workmanship. It is physics. And a strip of fibre mesh is what fixes it.

## Why the crack appears

Brick and concrete expand and shrink at different rates with heat and damp. Where they meet, they move against each other. Plaster that bridges the joint is stretched, and it cracks along the line.

The crack repeats on every joint in the building, which is why it looks so systematic.

## What fibre mesh does

Fibre mesh is a net of glass fibre coated to resist the alkali in cement. Embedded in the plaster, it spreads the stress over a wider strip so no single line takes the full pull. The plaster still moves, but it does not open into a visible crack.

## Where to use it

- **At brick and concrete joints:** beams, columns, slab edges.
- **Over conduit and pipe chases** that have been cut and filled.
- **At corners of windows and doors,** where diagonal cracks start.
- **Over whole walls** in thin plaster, in the form of a wide mesh.
- **On ceilings** where hairline cracks appear after the pour.

## Which width

| Width | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 4 inch | Narrow joints between brick and concrete |
| 6 inch | Beam and column joints, most common |
| 8 inch | Wide chases and corner reinforcement |
| 1 metre | Full wall or ceiling reinforcement |

## How to apply

1. **Clean the joint** and wet the surface.
2. **Apply the first coat** of plaster, about two-thirds of the final thickness.
3. **Press the mesh into the wet plaster**, centred on the joint, with at least 2 inches of overlap on each side.
4. **Cover it** with the second coat so the mesh is fully buried.
5. **Finish** the surface in the normal way.

Do not leave the mesh on the surface. A mesh that sits on top of the plaster does nothing and shows through the paint.

## Self-adhesive tape or plain mesh

- **Self-adhesive tape** is quick for dry joints and for plasterboard. It sticks to the wall.
- **Plain mesh** is pressed into wet plaster and is the choice for sand-cement plaster.

For brick and concrete in Indore, plain alkali-resistant mesh in plaster is the usual choice.

## Quality check

- **GSM:** heavier mesh, such as 145 gsm, is stronger than 45 gsm. Ask for the weight per square metre.
- **Alkali resistant coating:** a mesh without it breaks down in cement within a few years.
- **Weave:** even and tight, not loose.

## FAQs

**Does fibre mesh stop all plaster cracks?**  
It stops cracks caused by movement at joints. It does not fix structural movement or bad plaster mix.

**What width fibre mesh for a beam?**  
6 inch is the usual width, with 8 inch for wide joints.

**Is fibre mesh the same as wire mesh?**  
No. Fibre mesh is glass fibre, light and rust free. Wire mesh is steel and used for heavier plastering such as chicken mesh on timber.

**Can fibre mesh be used outdoors?**  
Yes, alkali resistant mesh embedded in cement plaster is used on external walls.

**How much fibre mesh do I need?**  
Measure the total length of beam, column and chase joints. Add 10% for overlap.
