Most people buy mosquito net the way they buy a bucket. They ask for the cheapest roll and find out about the difference two monsoons later, when the corners have rusted through or the mesh has gone brittle in the sun.

The material you pick decides how long the net lasts and what it does for your airflow. Here is how the four common types compare.

## The four materials

### Fibreglass (PVC coated glass fibre)

The everyday choice for house windows. Woven glass fibre strands are coated in PVC, so the mesh is soft, light and cannot rust. It cuts with ordinary scissors and fits into a plastic or aluminium frame with a spline.

- **Good for:** bedrooms, living rooms, normal window frames.
- **Watch for:** tears from pets, sharp objects or children. It sags if the frame is not pulled tight.
- **Colours:** grey, black, green and white.

### Stainless steel (SS) wire mesh

Fine woven steel wire. Much tougher than fibreglass. It resists chewing, cats and rats, and it shrugs off heat and wind.

- **Good for:** kitchens near the gas, ground floor windows, open balconies, anywhere pests are bigger than mosquitoes.
- **Watch for:** the price, and very fine mesh can cut airflow slightly. Ask for the wire grade. SS 304 is the one you want for outdoors.
- See [SS fine mesh](/products/ss-fine-mesh).

### Aluminium wire mesh

Soft, bright and does not rust. It is lighter than steel and a good middle option.

- **Good for:** humid rooms and balconies where rust is the problem.
- **Watch for:** it dents and creases more easily than steel.
- See [aluminium wiremesh](/products/aluminium-wiremesh).

### GI (galvanised iron) mesh

Mild steel with a zinc coat. The cheapest metal option and the usual choice for coolers and rough work.

- **Good for:** desert coolers, godowns, vents, areas where cost matters more than looks.
- **Watch for:** the zinc coat wears. Near the sea or in a damp bathroom it will rust in a few seasons.
- See [GI wiremesh](/products/gi-wiremesh).

## Mesh count: what 16 x 16 means

Mesh count is the number of wires per inch in each direction. Higher means finer holes and better protection against small insects, but also less airflow.

| Use | Typical mesh |
|---|---|
| Normal windows and doors | 16 x 16 or 18 x 16 |
| Sandflies and midges | 20 x 20 and finer |
| Pests, rats, lizards | Heavier SS or GI wire |

Ask the shop to show the roll labelled. If a seller cannot tell you the count, do not buy it.

## Buying checklist

1. **Measure the opening**, not the old net. Frames are rarely square.
2. **Add 2 inches on every side** for the spline and the pull.
3. **Ask for the roll width** and match it so you cut one clean piece, not two.
4. **Check the weave.** It should be even. Loose or wavy weave becomes a sag.
5. **Buy a spare metre.** The first cut is always a practice run.

## FAQs

**Which mosquito net lasts longest in Indore?**  
Stainless steel mesh lasts longest. Fibreglass lasts several seasons with care. GI mesh near damp areas rusts first.

**Can I fit a mosquito net myself?**  
Yes. A frame, a spline roller and scissors are enough for a window. Pull the mesh evenly, and trim after the spline is in.

**What mesh size stops mosquitoes?**  
16 x 16 and finer holds mosquitoes out. For sandflies pick 20 x 20 or finer.

**Do you cut mosquito net to size?**  
Yes. We cut on the counter by the metre or by the piece.

**Is fibreglass mesh safe for children?**  
It is non-toxic. It is also soft and can tear, so pick SS for the lower floor windows children lean on.
