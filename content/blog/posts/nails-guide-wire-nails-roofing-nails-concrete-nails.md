A nail looks like the simplest thing in the shop. Then a tin roof lifts in the first storm because someone used a wire nail where a roofing nail belonged.

Here is the short version of which nail goes where.

## The main types

### Wire nail (common nail)

Round, smooth, with a flat head. The general purpose nail. Used for framing, carpentry, shuttering and packing cases.

- **Lengths:** 1 inch to 6 inch.
- **Material:** mild steel, bright finish.
- See [wire nails](/products/fasteners-nails).

### Roofing nail

Has a wide head, often with a rubber or metal washer, and a twisted or ring shank that grips. Made for fixing GI and tin sheets on a roof without the head pulling through.

- **Lengths:** 1.5 inch to 6 inch.
- **Look for:** a wide head, a washer and a galvanised coat.
- See [roofing nails](/products/roofing-nail).

### Concrete (masonry) nail

Hardened steel, fluted or grooved, short and stubby. Hammered into brick, block and cured concrete without a drill.

- Wear eye protection. The steel is hard and can chip.

### Clout nail and tacks

Short, with a wide, flat head. For felt, upholstery, cartons and leather.

## Length: the rule of three

A nail should go through the thing you are fixing and into the base by about twice the thickness of the top piece.

- **Nail length = thickness of the top piece + about 2 × that thickness into the base.**

For a 1 inch plank fixed to a post, use 3 inch nails.

## Which nail, which job

| Job | Nail |
|---|---|
| Shuttering and formwork | 2 to 4 inch wire nail, plain |
| Carpentry, doors, frames | 2 to 3 inch wire nail |
| Wooden packing box | 1.5 to 2 inch wire nail |
| GI or tin roof sheets | Roofing nail with washer |
| Asbestos or cement sheet | Roofing nail with J-bolt or hook, as the roof demands |
| Brick wall, block wall | Concrete nail |
| False ceiling frames | 1.5 inch wire nail or clout |
| Fixing wire on a post | U-nail (staple) |

## Gauge: thin or thick

Nails come in gauges like wire. Thick nails hold more but split thin wood. If wood splits, go one gauge thinner, or blunt the point slightly with a tap of the hammer.

## Plain, galvanised or coated

- **Plain bright** for dry indoor work.
- **Galvanised** for outdoors, roofs and damp work.
- **Cement coated** for shuttering that must hold under wet concrete.

## Sold by weight

Nails are sold by the kilo. Heavier nails mean fewer per kilo. So before you compare prices, compare the number of nails in a kilo of each length.

| Size | Nails per kg (approx.) |
|---|---|

| 2 inch | 250 to 450 |

## FAQs

**What is the difference between a wire nail and a roofing nail?**  
A wire nail has a small flat head and a smooth shank. A roofing nail has a wide head, usually with a washer, so it holds a thin sheet down without tearing through.

**Which nail for a concrete wall?**  
A hardened concrete nail. For a long term hold use a plug and screw instead.

**How long should a nail be?**  
Long enough to go through the top piece and about twice that thickness into the base.

**Do you sell nails by the kilo?**  
Yes. We weigh them on the counter, any length from 1 to 6 inch.

**Are galvanised nails worth it?**  
For outdoor and roof work, yes. The zinc coat stops rust at the head and shank.
